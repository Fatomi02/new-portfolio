## The problem

Traffic enforcement in Nigeria depends heavily on an officer being present at
the moment an offence happens. Most dangerous driving is seen by other road
users and then forgotten, because there is nowhere useful to report it. The
plate number is right there on the back of the vehicle — the missing piece is
anything connecting that number to a record an enforcement body can act on.

This was my final-year research project at LAUTECH: build a database of
registered vehicle plate numbers and a reporting system on top of it, so a
member of the public can identify a vehicle and file a report that actually
reaches the Nigeria Police Force and the Federal Road Safety Corps.

## What I built

A web application with two halves.

The first is the lookup: a searchable database of registered plate numbers
that resolves a plate to its vehicle record.

The second is the reporting flow. A member of the public submits an offence
against a plate number, and the report is routed to the police and the FRSC
rather than landing in an inbox nobody reads.

## The design problem: reporting is easy to abuse

A system that lets anyone file a report against any plate number is a system
that can be used to harass people. This was the part of the project that
needed the most thought, and it is the part worth talking about in an
interview.
